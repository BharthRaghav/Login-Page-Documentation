# Online Banking — Business Analysis Case Study
## Project Overview
This project demonstrates an end-to-end Business Analysis workflow for an Online Banking enhancement. The objective is to document how customers can access online banking through a login and new-user registration process, supported by clearly defined functional requirements, business rules, process flows, and test scenarios.
The project focuses on translating business needs into structured documentation that can help business stakeholders, developers, and QA teams understand the expected system behavior.

**Project type**: Business Analysis Portfolio Case Study.

**Domain:** Banking / Financial Services

**Primary focus**: Requirements Engineering, Login & Registration Workflows, Functional Documentation, and Test Case Design.

_This is a portfolio case study based on a banking scenario, not an official Bank of America project or an implementation of its production systems._

## 1. Business Problem
The business scenario identifies one key challenges:

**Limited service availability:** Customers have restricted access to traditional banking services outside business hours.

### Proposed Solution
Introduce online banking capabilities that allow customers to access their accounts 24/7, with the broader goal of improving customer convenience and reducing dependence on contact-center support.

## 2. Project Objectives
-Translate business needs into functional requirements.

-Document the login and new-user registration workflows.

-Define input rules and how the system should respond to errors.

-Describe normal, alternative, and exception flows.

-Prepare test scenarios to validate expected system behavior.

-Organize project documents so business, development, and testing teams can understand them.

Business Analysis Deliverables
### 1. Business Requirements Document (BRD)
Documents the business problem, proposed solution, impacted system, and high-level business requirements.

**Purpose:** Establish what the business needs to achieve and why the proposed solution is required.

### 2. Functional Requirements Document (FRD)
Describes the expected system behavior for the online banking entry points, user ID validation, new user registration, password validation, and related navigation.

**Purpose:** Convert high-level business needs into detailed, testable system requirements.

### 3. Login Use Case
Documents the interaction between a registered online banking user and the system.

It covers the primary login flow, preconditions, postconditions, alternate scenarios, and exception handling.

**Purpose:** Explain how the user and system interact step by step.

### 4. UML Activity Diagram
Represents the login workflow visually, including decision points for user ID validation, password validation, and account locking.

**Purpose:** Make the process flow and alternative paths easier to understand.

### 5. Login Test Cases

Documents test scenarios, actions, and expected results for login behavior.

The scenarios include:

-Successful login.

-Invalid user ID.

-Invalid password.

-Account locking after repeated failed password attempts.

-Login attempt with an already locked account.
